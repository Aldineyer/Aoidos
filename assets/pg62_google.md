## "The Immortal Soldier's Confession"

### AUDIO PROFILE

The narrator is John Carter, better known as Captain Jack Carter of Virginia, a former Confederate cavalry officer and seasoned soldier of fortune. He is an ageless man, reflecting on his inexplicably long life with the weary gravity of someone who has died twice yet still fears death. The recommended voice for this profile is **Algenib** (Gravelly) or **Gacrux** (Mature).

## THE SCENE: The Hidden Study

A dimly lit, quiet room late at night. The atmosphere is heavy with the weight of untold secrets, serving as the environmental context for the interaction. The narrator is alone, setting down a chronicle that he intends no mortal man to read until after his final death. The vibe is eerie, solemn, and deeply introspective.

### DIRECTOR'S NOTES

* **Style:** Serious, reflective, and slightly melancholic. The delivery should sound like a deeply personal, almost reluctant confession from a man burdened by an unbelievable truth.


* **Pacing:** Deliberate and unhurried. The tempo should slow down significantly on the philosophical points about life and death to emphasize the character's existential dread.


* **Accent:** A refined, polite, but weathered Southern American accent, specifically that of a Virginia gentleman.



### SAMPLE CONTEXT

John Carter is recording the prologue to his secret manuscript. He is establishing his inexplicable agelessness and hinting at the strange events that occurred while his body lay undiscovered in an Arizona cave for ten years.

#### TRANSCRIPT

`[sighs]` I am a very old man; how old I do not know. `[serious]` Possibly I am a hundred, possibly more; but I cannot tell because I have never aged as other men, nor do I remember any childhood. So far as I can recollect I have always been a man, a man of about thirty. `[tired]` I appear today as I did forty years and more ago, and yet I feel that I cannot go on living forever; that some day I shall die the real death from which there is no resurrection.

`[whispers]` I do not know why I should fear death, I who have died twice and am still alive; `[serious]` but yet I have the same horror of it as you who have never died, and it is because of this terror of death, I believe, that I am so convinced of my mortality.

And because of this conviction I have determined to write down the story of the interesting periods of my life and of my death. I cannot explain the phenomena; `[curious]` I can only set down here in the words of an ordinary soldier of fortune a chronicle of the strange events that befell me during the ten years that my dead body lay undiscovered in an Arizona cave.

I have never told this story, nor shall mortal man see this manuscript until after I have passed over for eternity. `[confident]` I know that the average human mind will not believe what it cannot grasp, and so I do not purpose being pilloried by the public, the pulpit, and the press, and held up as a colossal liar when I am but telling the simple truths which some day science will substantiate. `[amazed]` Possibly the suggestions which I gained upon Mars, and the knowledge which I can set down in this chronicle, will aid in an earlier understanding of the mysteries of our sister planet; `[whispers]` mysteries to you, but no longer mysteries to me.
