### Terminology

### 1. The General Terms
* **Audiobook Industry**
* **The Script:** This is the most common industry term used by voice actors, sound engineers, and directors during recording sessions (e.g., *"Let's take it from page 4 of the script"*).
* **The Manuscript (or Source Text):** The traditional publishing term for the complete written work provided by the author or publisher.
* **Booth**

### 2. The Narrator’s Working Copy/Version
* **Prepped Script / Marked-Up Manuscript:** 
  * narrator’s personalized version. 
  * It is usually loaded onto a silent tablet (via apps like iAnnotate or GoodNotes) and marked with:
  * Pronunciation guides (for unusual names, foreign words, or fictional terms).
  * Character voice tags (accents, pitch, pacing notes).
  * Breathing points, pauses, and emotional beats.
  * Pacing and emphasis indicators.
The annotated working version used in the booth. 
The narrator's personal working copy, annotated with pronunciation keys, character voice tags, tone changes, pauses, and accent markers before recording.

### 3. Specialized Industry Variations
* **ARC (Advance Reader Copy) or Galleys:** If an audiobook is being recorded *before* the physical book is published, the narrator is often reading from an ARC or digital galley proofs (which may still contain minor typos).
* **Audio-Adapted Script:** In some non-fiction or heavily visual books, the text must be modified for audio (e.g., changing *"as shown in the diagram below"* to *"as described here"* or writing out visual charts into spoken descriptions).
* **Pronunciation/Character Sheet (Accompanying document):** While not the full text, narrators often work with a companion document listing every character, their description, and verified pronunciations approved by the author or rights holder.

### Summary
* If referring to the **file received from the author/publisher**: It’s the **Manuscript** (or **Source Text**).
* If referring to the **text read in the booth**: It’s the **Script**.
* If referring to the **annotated copy with narrator notes**: It’s the **Prepped (or Marked-Up) Manuscript/Script**.

 Transforming a fiction manuscript into a fully produced, professional audiobook is a multi-step pipeline combining creative performance, audio engineering, and project management. 

Here is the complete, industry-standard workflow from text to final distribution.

### Phase 1: Pre-Production (Planning & Casting)

#### 1. Rights & Route Selection
* **Rights Verification:** Ensure audio rights are clear.
* **Production Path:**
  * **Traditional/Publisher:** Handled by an audio publisher (Audible Studios, Tantor, Macmillan Audio).
  * **Independent (Indie):** The author acts as executive producer via platforms like ACX (Audible), Findaway Voices, or direct hire.
* **Payment Structure:** Negotiated as **PFH** (Per Finished Hour, e.g., $150–$400+/hour), **Royalty Share**, or a hybrid.

#### 2. Casting the Narrator(s)
* **Performance Style:**
  * **Solo Narrator:** One actor voices the entire book, including all male/female/creature voices. (Most common).
  * **Dual Narration:** Two narrators alternate full chapters (common in romance/dual-POV).
  * **Duet Narration:** Narrators perform character lines collaboratively across chapters.
  * **Full Cast / Audio Drama:** Dedicated actors per character with sound effects/music.
* **Auditions:** Narrators submit 2–3 minute sample reads featuring both narrative tone and a scene with dialogue switching between distinct characters.

#### 3. Manuscript Prep (Prepping the Book)
* **First Read-Through:** The narrator (or a dedicated prep assistant) reads the entire book to understand emotional arcs, plot twists, and character origins.
* **Pronunciation Research & Sheet:** A spreadsheet is created tracking fantasy names, foreign words, historical accents, and place names. The author/rights holder signs off on this.
* **Character Voice Bible:** A reference guide documenting each character's age, pitch, accent, tone, and personality quirks.
* **Script Markup:** The manuscript is loaded onto a silent tablet and marked up with breaths, emphasis, character color-coding, and tone shifts.

---

### Phase 2: Production (Recording the Audio)

#### 4. Studio Recording (Tracking)
* **Environment:** A sound-treated, soundproofed home studio or professional recording facility with a high-end condenser/shotgun microphone and an ultra-low noise floor (under -60dB).
* **Recording Methods:**
  * **Punch & Roll (Standard):** When the narrator makes an error, they pause, back the track up to the start of the sentence, and "punch in" seamlessly. This produces cleaner raw audio.
  * **Marker/Clicker Method:** The narrator uses a dog clicker or visual marker to flag mistakes and re-reads the line without stopping, leaving the clean-up to the editor.
* **Time Ratio:** It takes roughly **1.5 to 2.5 hours of studio time** to record **1 finished hour** of finished audio.

---

### Phase 3: Post-Production (Editing, Proofing, & Mastering)

#### 5. Audio Editing
* A specialized dialogue editor cleans the raw audio track:
  * Removes mouth clicks, spit sounds, throat noise, and distracting breaths.
  * Removes stomach rumbles, outside noise bleed, or accidental mic bumps.
  * Adjusts pacing between sentences, dialogue exchanges, and paragraph transitions.

#### 6. Proofing (Audio Quality Control)
* An **Audio Proofer** listens to the edited audio from start to finish while reading along with the manuscript to log:
  * **Word misses:** Omissions, additions, or substituted words (e.g., saying "house" instead of "horse").
  * **Mispronunciations:** Deviation from the approved pronunciation sheet.
  * **Character Voice Drift:** A character accidentally slipping from an accent or using the wrong voice.
  * **Audio Artifacts:** Missed background noises or abrupt edits.

#### 7. Pickups (Corrections)
* The narrator returns to the booth to re-record every line flagged during proofing.
* Crucial detail: The narrator must match their exact microphone position, room acoustics, gain levels, and vocal energy from the original session.
* The editor splices the corrected lines back into the master timeline.

#### 8. Mastering (Final Polish & Technical Compliance)
* A mastering engineer processes the final audio through EQ, light compression, and limiting to ensure it meets strict platform specifications:
  * **RMS (Average Volume):** -18dB to -23dB.
  * **Peak Levels:** Max -3.0dB (to prevent distortion).
  * **Noise Floor:** Lower than -60dB.
  * **File Formats:** 192kbps or higher MP3, 44.1 kHz, CBR (Constant Bitrate).
  * **Room Tone Formatting:** Exact heads (0.5–1 second) and tails (1–5 seconds) of silence per chapter.

---

### Phase 4: Packaging & Distribution

#### 9. Asset Preparation
* **Audio Split:** Divided into exact chapters, plus Opening Credits, Closing Credits, and a Retail Sample (typically a compelling 1–5 minute excerpt).
* **Audiobook Cover Art:** Created as a square 1:1 ratio (usually 2400 x 2400 to 3200 x 3200 pixels) and distinct from standard vertical print covers.

#### 10. Ingestion, QA, & Publication
* Audio files and metadata are uploaded to a distributor:
  * **ACX:** Distributes directly to Audible, Amazon, and Apple Books.
  * **Aggregators (Findaway Voices, Authors Republic, InAudio):** Distributes wide to 40+ platforms (Spotify, Kobo, Storytel, libraries via OverDrive/Libby and Hoopla).
* The distributor runs an automated and manual QA inspection.
* Once approved, the audiobook goes live for sale (turnaround typically takes **10 business days to 4 weeks** depending on the platform).

---

### Quick Reference: Standard Book Math
* **Average Reading Speed:** ~9,300 words = **1 Finished Hour (PFH)**.
* **Average 80,000-word novel:**
  * **Final Audio Length:** ~8.5 hours.
  * **Booth Recording Time:** ~15–18 hours.
  * **Total Production Time (Edit/Proof/Master):** ~30–45 hours.

Here is a detailed breakdown of the software ecosystem used at every stage of audiobook production, with the specific inputs flowing in and outputs flowing out of each step.

---

## Phase 1: Pre-Production

### 1A. Manuscript Reading & Markup

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Final manuscript from publisher/author (PDF, DOCX, or EPUB) |
| **Software** | **iAnnotate** (industry standard for narrators on iPad), **GoodNotes**, **Notability**, **Adobe Acrobat Reader**, **Kindle app** (for a first casual read-through) |
| **What it does** | Narrator reads and annotates the PDF directly on a silent tablet: color-coding characters, marking breath points, flagging tricky passages, noting emotional beats |
| **Outputs** | Annotated/prepped PDF script (the narrator's booth copy) |

### 1B. Pronunciation & Character Research

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Manuscript text, author notes, any existing franchise glossaries |
| **Software** | **Microsoft Excel** or **Google Sheets** (most common), **Airtable**, **Notion** |
| **What it does** | Builds a searchable pronunciation guide and character voice bible; often shared with the author/rights holder for approval via **Google Docs** or **Dropbox Paper** |
| **Outputs** | Approved Pronunciation Sheet (.xlsx / .csv) and Character Voice Bible (.docx / .pdf) |

---

## Phase 2: Production (Recording)

### 2A. Audio Tracking (The Booth Session)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Prepped manuscript (PDF on tablet), microphone + audio interface (hardware), room tone sample |
| **Software (DAWs)** | **Reaper** (dominant in indie audiobook production due to low cost and powerful scripting), **Pro Tools** (industry standard in traditional publishing studios), **Adobe Audition**, **Studio One**, **Logic Pro** (Mac users), **Audacity** (free/budget, limited punch-and-roll) |
| **Key DAW Features Used** | **Punch & Roll** (Reaper, Pro Tools, Audition, Studio One all support this natively), **Markers/Regions** for chapter breaks, **Loudness meters** for real-time level monitoring |
| **Outputs** | Raw session files: multitrack or single-track **WAV files** (typically **48 kHz / 24-bit**), one file per chapter or per recording session. Also includes a **room tone** recording (30–60 seconds of silence in the booth) |

---

## Phase 3: Post-Production

### 3A. Audio Editing (Cleaning & Pacing)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Raw WAV session files from the recording phase, room tone file |
| **Software (DAW)** | Same DAW as recording (most commonly **Reaper** or **Pro Tools**) — editing is almost always done in the same environment where tracking occurred |
| **Specialized Repair Software** | **iZotope RX** (the industry gold standard for spectral audio repair): used for **Mouth De-Click**, **De-Plosive**, **De-Ess**, **Spectral De-Noise**, **De-Reverb**, and **Breath Control**. **Acon Digital DeVerberate** and **Supertone Clear** are also used |
| **What it does** | Removes mouth clicks, lip smacks, stomach rumbles, plosives, sibilance, chair squeaks, and background noise. Adjusts silence gaps between sentences and paragraphs to natural pacing. Splices in room tone to fill unnatural gaps |
| **Outputs** | Cleaned, paced **WAV files** per chapter (still at 48 kHz / 24-bit) |

### 3B. Audio Proofing (Quality Control)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Edited WAV files + original manuscript (PDF or DOCX) |
| **Software** | **Reaper** or **Pro Tools** (proofer listens in the DAW and drops markers at errors), **VLC** or **foobar2000** (simpler playback), **Google Sheets** or **Excel** (error log), sometimes **Frame.io** or **Splice** for timestamped review notes |
| **What it does** | Proofer listens word-for-word against the manuscript, logging every error with a timestamp |
| **Outputs** | **Pickup List / Error Log** (.xlsx or .docx) containing: timestamp, error type (misread, mispronunciation, noise, character drift), and the correct text |

### 3C. Pickups (Re-Recording Corrections)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Pickup List, original session files (to match mic placement, gain, and room acoustics) |
| **Software** | Same DAW as original recording session (**Reaper**, **Pro Tools**, etc.) |
| **What it does** | Narrator re-records flagged lines. Editor crossfades/splices the new takes into the master timeline, matching volume and room tone |
| **Outputs** | Updated, corrected **WAV files** per chapter |

### 3D. Mastering (Final Polish & Compliance)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Final corrected WAV files (48 kHz / 24-bit), room tone file |
| **Software (DAW)** | **Reaper**, **Pro Tools**, **Adobe Audition**, **Studio One** |
| **Mastering Plugins** | **iZotope Ozone** (EQ, dynamics, loudness), **FabFilter Pro-L 2** (limiting), **Waves L2 / L3 Ultramaximizer**, **iZotope RX Loudness Control** |
| **Compliance Checking** | **ACX Check** (free plugin for Audacity and Reaper that automatically validates Audible's technical specs), **YouLean Loudness Meter** (free, for LUFS measurement), **dpMeter** |
| **What it does** | Applies subtle EQ to smooth tonal balance, light compression to even out dynamics, and a limiter to control peaks. Normalizes to platform loudness targets. Adds required head/tail silence. Converts sample rate and bit depth |
| **Outputs** | Final mastered files: **192 kbps+ MP3, 44.1 kHz, CBR, mono**, with RMS between **-18 dB and -23 dB**, peaks no higher than **-3.0 dB**, noise floor below **-60 dB**. Each chapter file includes **0.5–1 sec head silence** and **1–5 sec tail silence** |

---

## Phase 4: Packaging & Distribution

### 4A. File Preparation & Metadata

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Mastered MP3 chapter files, cover art, book metadata (title, author, narrator, description, ISBN) |
| **Software** | **Mp3tag** or **Kid3** (for embedding ID3 metadata tags into MP3 files), **Adobe Photoshop** or **Canva** (for resizing cover art to 2400×2400 px square), **FFmpeg** (command-line batch conversion if needed) |
| **Outputs** | Tagged MP3 files with embedded metadata, square cover art JPEG (2400×2400 to 3200×3200 px, 72+ DPI, RGB) |

### 4B. Upload & Distribution

| | |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | Final MP3 files, cover art, metadata, retail audio sample (1–5 min excerpt) |
| **Software (Web Dashboards)** | **ACX** (Audible/Amazon/Apple Books), **Findaway Voices** (wide distribution to 40+ platforms including Spotify, Kobo, Storytel, libraries via OverDrive/Libby/Hoopla), **Authors Republic**, **InAudio**, **PublishDrive** |
| **What it does** | Upload files, enter metadata, set pricing/territories, submit for platform QA review |
| **Outputs** | Published audiobook live on retail and library platforms (typically **10 business days to 4 weeks** after submission) |

### A Note on the "Standard Stack"

If you were to ask a working indie audiobook narrator what their daily setup looks like, the most common answer would be:

> **Reaper** (DAW) + **iZotope RX** (repair) + **iZotope Ozone** (mastering) + **ACX Check** (compliance) + **iAnnotate** on iPad (script) + **Findaway Voices** (distribution)

This combination covers every stage at a relatively low cost and is the de facto standard in the independent audiobook community. Traditional publishers tend to substitute **Pro Tools** for Reaper and may use in-house proprietary QC systems.
